# Visual logic has several forms

Use this guide when planning relationships within and between shots. The brief and observed reference choose the organizing rules. A single evolving object is one option, never the definition of a coherent video. Intentional montage, changes of subject, discontinuities and contrast are valid.

## Identify what connects the actual pictures

Observe outgoing and incoming frames, intermediate frames if any, and the recurring sequence. Consider the relationships below only when relevant:

| Relationship | What to specify in this job | What the reviewer should see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Composition / shape | Matching position, contour, axis, scale or negative space | The eye can locate the handoff even when subjects change |
| Motion / direction | Outgoing momentum and incoming continuation, reversal or interruption | A purposeful directional relation rather than unrelated jumps |
| Material / representation | A photo reused in a crop, mask, collage, surface, point field or graphic layout | Recognizable information carried into a different visual form |
| Depth / framing | Foreground, background and focal point change roles; a detail becomes a scene | A motivated change in spatial attention |
| Graphic / typography | Text, line, shape or layout supplies the bridge or recurring rule | Graphic elements organize the composition rather than decorate it randomly |
| Recurrence / variation | A motif or shot family returns with a changed crop, treatment, arrangement or role | Recognition plus a meaningful difference, not compulsory restoration |
| Theme / association | Different assets form an authored comparison or progression | The relationship can be described concretely and is visible in the selection/order |
| Contrast / interruption | A deliberate break in density, space, color, subject or movement | The contrast serves the brief and subsequent pattern; a cut is allowed |

This is a vocabulary, not an effect menu or checklist requiring every type. A shared palette alone may connect some works but cannot stand in for the reference's observed choreography. A persistent anchor may be a recurring word, geometry, axis, treatment, motif or theme; it need not be an unchanged physical object. If the reference's anchor is a rule rather than an object, name that rule.

## Plan at three scales

1. **Whole sequence:** what shot families alternate, recur or build; where changes in density matter; what the ending does with earlier material.
2. **Shot relationship:** use the film plan's existing `entry.reason`, `entry.carries` and `entry.changes` to describe the actual bridge or intentional contrast. A hard cut can be correct. Do not write only "on the beat" or "same style."
3. **Within the shot:** distinguish primary motion, supporting layers and transient events. Layers may enter, overlap and exit at different times. Density is not just cut count: several coordinated operations may happen inside one shot.

Within a shot, repeated instances, staggered movement, scale/depth progression and controlled overlaps can create an organized field. Do not equate all repeated elements with a new cut or reduce layered choreography to a single global sine wave. These remain choices derived from the reference rather than universal requirements.

Also track **role handoffs**: a supporting line, shape, material fragment or image region can become the next focal point, aperture, surface or environment. The visible relationship matters; do not claim that two unrelated meshes physically morphed merely because their edits overlap. An overlay can bridge a hard background cut. Conversely, a full-frame flash or a sudden replacement can intentionally punctuate the sequence without a bridge on every boundary.

Material transformations can be substantial: crop, silhouette, recoloring, reflective sampling, repeated facets or source-derived fragments may change the source's recognizability. Preserve the level of recognition actually required by the brief/reference. Do not insist that every asset stay intact and readable throughout, and do not replace it with generic geometry when identifiable source information is required. For extreme passages, trace which source information is carried forward and which is deliberately lost, then review that result.

When multiple references are supplied as examples of one author's work, compare their actual passages to identify recurring operations and their range: graphic/live-action alternation, hierarchy changes, controlled interruption, density contrasts, reflection, fragmentation, recurrence or other observed operations. Keep this common grammar separate from each clip's palette, props, layout and particular sequence. Authorship information supplied by the user does not prove that every clip uses every operation. Do not turn the comparison into a fixed effect order or insist that all future works use the same transition.

Keep bridge duration, overlap, movement direction, recurrence and perceptual hierarchy in the job's motion description. When adapting a fast reference to another song, retain appropriate relative density and layered choreography; choose action durations and measured target triggers for the new song rather than pasting source timestamps or uniformly slowing the entire edit. Sparse timing use must not erase the reference's defining density. Conversely, do not activate every musical event merely to look busy.

## Review the relationship, not just continuity

Use native-speed playback for pace and slowed playback/native frames for construction. Inspect several consecutive boundaries and at least one return when recurrence is defining. Can the viewer follow the designed association, contrast or handoff? Does each layer have a role? Is the fast passage internally evolving, or just cycling stills? Does the return recall earlier material with the intended change?

In `sequence_reviews.checks.continuity`, judge the chosen visual organization. Do not mark a purposeful subject change or abrupt cut as a failure merely because physical continuity is absent. Do not pass a dull continuous shot merely because one object stays visible. Retain unresolved density, hierarchy and material-treatment mismatches even when cue times are correct.
